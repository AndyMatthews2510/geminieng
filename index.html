Audience & Medium Adaptation Matrix

Professional engineering communication is not one-size-fits-all. The table below illustrates how narrative strategy, data granularity, and flaw handling must adapt across each target venue.

Communication Venue Target Audience Primary Rhetorical Goal Data Granularity & Style Handling Flaws & Anomalies
Archival Journal Article
Written Manuscript		 International domain peers, reviewers, and practitioners. Establish an original, peer-reviewed technical contribution with full reproducibility. Exhaustive specification of models, tolerances, governing equations, and error bars. Characterized rigorously through uncertainty analysis and scope boundaries.
Lab Progress Review
Oral / Slide Deck		 Faculty advisors, lab mentors, peer researchers. Solicit troubleshooting advice, confirm direction, and unblock active research dead-ends. Focused on immediate experimental milestones, test setups, and active failure points. Center-stage: Highlight anomalies and failed trials to receive diagnostic guidance.
Academic Conference
Oral / 12-Min Talk		 Specialized domain researchers, session chairs, peer critics. Disseminate validated findings and establish priority within a strict 12-minute window. Intuitive block diagrams, high-contrast trend curves; minimal raw algebra. Frankly acknowledged as operational envelope limits and stated future work.
Management Pitch
Executive Briefing		 Engineering directors, plant managers, capital allocators. Secure budget, project greenlight, equipment acquisition, or line change approval. Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF): financial ROI, downtime risk, and throughput gain. Framed as identified business/safety risks with active mitigation contingencies.

Course Integration: Recommended Semester Progression

Instructors and students can integrate both portals across a typical 15-week senior design capstone, undergraduate research thesis, or advanced laboratory curriculum:

Weeks 1 – 4
Scope & Literature
Use the Manuscript Portal to study the CARS model, analyze target journal papers, and structure problem-niche statements.
Weeks 5 – 8
Progress Gate Reviews
Use the Presentation Portal (Scenario A) to structure mid-term advisor check-ins, surfacing technical roadblocks and test anomalies.
Weeks 9 – 12
Drafting & Peer Review
Apply the Critique Lab and 100-Point Journal Rubric for reciprocal student peer evaluations of Methods and Results sections.
Weeks 13 – 15
Symposium & Defense
Prepare conference talks or executive pitches using the Slide Redesign Studio, Delivery Rubric, and Q&A Defense.
